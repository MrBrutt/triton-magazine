title: Наш близкий далекий учитель
page: 18

Новый учитель нашей Гимназии, Питер Уэст (Peter West), – персона загадочная и харизматичная. Мы захотели взять у него интервью, чтобы узнать немного больше о его богатой личности и интересном жизненном опыте.

{en} (1) Uma: You've started working at our school this year. So, what could you say about the gymnasium and its students?

{en} Peter: Well, it’s a very welcoming place, and I felt like I was able to start working straight away, somehow. Also, cool kids. To give my personal assessment, I feel like in other schools I’ve worked at, about 50% of kids cared, but I think here it’s about 95%, which is really high, and it’s really a credit to the school.

Мне кажется, в других школах, где я работал, примерно только половина ребят заботилась об учебе, но здесь это число достигает 95%, и это действительно заслуга школы.

{en} (2) U: We’ve heard that you used to work in other countries. Could you please tell us more about that?

{en} P: I’ve worked in Germany for 12 years, and I enjoyed that. Although, in saying that, I like Russia more, and I wasn’t expecting that. I feel really good here. I’ve also worked in England for 5 years, and I’ve worked in Australia for 5-7 years. And Australia, for me, is a bit boring.

{en} (3) U: Are there any cultural differences between those countries?

{en} P: Well, sure, of course there are. Germany has a reputation for being a bit quiet and reserved, and it was. It was difficult to make friends sometimes, but, in saying that, the friends that I made there are somehow very deep. The negative part of that is that it’s hard to form friendships; it takes a long time. But the friendships that you’ve formed seem to last forever. In saying that, I don’t know why, but it seems a bit easier to make friends in Russia.

В Германии люди довольно сдержанные, и это не так уж просто завести там друзей. Но с теми друзьями, с кем у меня получилось построить отношения, у нас очень глубокая прочная связь. Мне кажется, в России подружиться с людьми несколько легче, чем в Германии. (4) U: And what are the main differences in culture between Australia and Russia?

{en} P: Well, that’s an interesting question. I think when we live in Australia, especially when we’re on holidays, everybody is speaking to each other and saying ‘hello’ to each other, and it seems very friendly, whereas in Russia it seems to take some time to get that. But, on the playground, for example, where I live... I’ve been living there for 3 years now, and I started shaking people’s hands and meeting the neighbours, but it’s taking a couple of years. So, it seems as if Australia was a bit more open. Who knows, maybe just saying ‘hello’ is not important somehow, but it just seems to be a little easier to make this first connection with people. But when you make it with people in Russia, it’s more special. Maybe it’s a bit more... honest, somehow, if you know what I mean. Maybe it’s a bit deeper.

Живя в Австралии, я заметил, что все склонны здороваться и разговаривать друг с другом. Это выглядит очень дружелюбно. В России для такой коммуникации требуется больше времени. Я прожил 3 года, прежде чем начал общаться с соседями. Установить контакт с людьми в России – это нечто более глубокое и искреннее, чем простой «привет», которому в других местах не придают значение.

{en} (5) U: And the last question. Based on your experience, can you tell us about the differences between school education? In Australia, Germany, and Russia, for example.

{en} P: Well, that’s interesting… I think Australia (or where I’ve worked in Australia) is definitely more multicultural. There were many Lebanese kids, Indian kids, Vietnamese kids, Turkish kids, Filipino kids — kids from all over the world in Sydney, where I grew up, and I really loved that; it was multicultural! You could meet kids from everywhere. And it kind of got me interested in travel. It made me interested in India, for example, and I went there for a trip and then came to other places too, so I guess that Australia, especially in Sydney, was more multicultural.

{en} P: Let me say something about Skolkovo: I'm very impressed with this school. And the kids... I wasn't expecting the kids to be so kind to me because, you know, a foreign teacher is new and doesn't speak Russian completely. You know, I was wondering what was going to happen (and it's my first time in a Russian government school), but I really, really love it, and I love the kids at this school; as I've said, they're all really caring, as are the teachers, so I really love this school. I'm passionate about this school. I really love it, and I feel good here.

Позвольте, я скажу кое-что про Сколково: я крайне впечатлен этой школой.

Я никогда не думал, что ученики здесь будут настолько добры ко мне, ведь я новый иностранный учитель, и еще не владею свободно русским. Я по-настоящему полюбил эту школу и детей, которые здесь учатся. Повторюсь, дети, как и все учителя, здесь действительно крайне ответственны. Я просто влюблен в гимназию и чувствую себя здесь просто отлично.

{en} U: Thank you a lot. P: Thank you.
